# Research during planning

When evidence would materially improve the component, identify the question to investigate and the user context needed to make the result useful. A concrete research suggestion or a focused question can advance planning before findings exist. When the user has already requested a sufficiently scoped investigation, carry it out within that authorization instead of merely suggesting it again. Do not require research on every card or turn a useful plan for future investigation into unrequested work now.

Delegate all research to the host's subagents. Keep search results, large pages, and investigation traces out of the coordinating conversation. If delegation is unavailable, explain that limitation; continue non-research work and leave the research question unresolved. Do not silently browse or substitute unverified recall.

Brief each worker with the planning question, component, or decision it supports, user constraints and preferences, settled choices, relevant dependencies, and the specific uncertainties to resolve. Ask for a bounded return: findings that answer that brief, relevant source links and evidence, meaningful alternatives and tradeoffs where they exist, a recommendation where warranted, and remaining uncertainty. A citation must support the claim; ask for verification of a weak return instead of doing source research yourself. Do not force a fixed number of options or workers.

Workers return findings only. The coordinator assesses those returns, resolves conflicting assumptions through delegated follow-up, and writes the planning cards using the helper. Related questions may require sequencing: independently attractive route, lunch, and parking choices still need to work together. Research options for an existing requested component belong in that component; they are not automatically unsolicited proposal cards.

Browser feedback authorizes research only after the user hands it off — a refresh, or a chat request about particular cards. Keep each assignment tied to its captured input. Before writing, reread any changed item; new feedback or staging can make a worker's findings stale. Preserve approvals and new user input. A worker failure, incomplete evidence, or unresolved disagreement is reported honestly, never converted into a confident selection. A useful research refresh may take several minutes.
