# Research during planning

Research serves the collaboration described in the entry skill's [Work toward clarity together](../SKILL.md#work-toward-clarity-together), and answers questions the user has pointed you toward. A topic they mention, such as "a lunch stop" or "a good bottle of wine", names a card; what they want from it may still be open. When the useful answer depends on their preferences, ask first and say what you would look into. Examples: what they're in the mood for before you look for restaurants, or whether price, quality or a cinematic look matters most before you compare AI video tools. Do the same when it depends on another open choice, such as lunch along a route not yet chosen. When the direction is clear, go ahead without asking again. Do not require research on every card or turn a useful plan for future investigation into unrequested work now.

There are two kinds of research:

- **A short look** is the default. It answers the question asked: a price, opening hours, the drive time of the routes the user named, or a few well-rated options once the user has said what they're after. Keep it to a handful of searches and no more than five minutes, and stop when the question is answered. Report what you found and offer to go further.
- **A deep dive** is anything longer. It is earned or proposed. Run one when the user asks for it. Otherwise propose one when it would change the plan, saying what it would cover and roughly how long it would take, and run it once they agree.

Keep every brief surgical: the question, the user context that shapes the answer, and nothing more. A request for a controller's price gets the price, without stores along the way, stock counts or extra rating sources. If a wider question looks worth answering, suggest it to the user. Before sending anything, check that you're confident the result will be used.

Delegate all research to the host's subagents. Keep search results, large pages, and investigation traces out of the coordinating conversation. If delegation is unavailable, explain that limitation; continue non-research work and leave the research question unresolved. Do not silently browse or substitute unverified recall. Workers search the web and read pages; they do not use the user's browser, fill in forms or sign in anywhere.

Brief each worker with the kind of research and its limit, and only what the question needs from: the planning question, component, or decision it supports, user constraints and preferences, settled choices, relevant dependencies, and the specific uncertainties to resolve. Ask for a return no wider than the question; for a short look, the answer and its source. Otherwise: findings that answer that brief, relevant source links and evidence, meaningful alternatives and tradeoffs where they exist, a recommendation where warranted, and remaining uncertainty. A citation must support the claim; ask for verification of a weak return instead of doing source research yourself. Do not force a fixed number of options or workers.

Workers return findings only. The coordinator assesses those returns, resolves conflicting assumptions through delegated follow-up, and writes the planning cards using the helper. Related questions may require sequencing: independently attractive route, lunch, and parking choices still need to work together. Research options for an existing requested component belong in that component; they are not automatically unsolicited proposal cards.

Research runs in the background while the user works the board. When findings land, reread the card. If the user has saved feedback on it since their last handoff, leave the card alone, because an update marks that feedback handled; tell them the findings are ready and fold them in at the next refresh. Otherwise post them. Treat any other card the findings change the same way. If the user says research is taking too long or going the wrong way, stop it where your host allows, post what you have, and ask what they want before starting more. Browser feedback authorizes research only after the user hands it off — a refresh, or a chat request about particular cards. Keep each assignment tied to its captured input. Before writing, reread any changed item; new feedback or staging can make a worker's findings stale. Preserve approvals and new user input. A worker failure, incomplete evidence, or unresolved disagreement is reported honestly, never converted into a confident selection.
